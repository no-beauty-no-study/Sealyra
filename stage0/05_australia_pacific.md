# Stage 0, Chapter 5: Australia and the Pacific

---

## 5.1  The Continent That Drifted Alone

Australia tore loose from the supercontinent Gondwana very early in its geological history and drifted slowly northward across the surrounding ocean, encircled by sea barriers no foreign species could traverse. Within the long isolation that followed, every native lineage on the continent evolved without competition from outsiders, generating a flora and fauna without any equivalent anywhere else on Earth. The eucalyptus filled the forests. The acacia spread across the dry interior. Animals that on every other continent had long since been outcompeted by placental mammals were free here to occupy every available niche themselves, because the placental mammals had never managed to arrive at all. The result is a continent that biologically resembles no other on the planet, and which has therefore demonstrated, for tens of millions of years, exactly what evolution does when it is left strictly alone.

**Q1:** From which ancient supercontinent did Australia tear loose and drift slowly northward into geological isolation?
**A1:** Gondwana

**Q2:** Which dominant Australian tree filled the forests of the isolated continent in the absence of competing species?
**A2:** eucalyptus

**Q3:** Which mammal group was absent from Australia, allowing other lineages to occupy every available niche?
**A3:** placental mammal

## 5.2  The Pouched Continent

In the absence of placental mammals, the marsupials of Australia diversified to fill every ecological niche on the continent themselves. Koalas ascended into the eucalyptus canopy and developed an entire metabolism specialised to digest its toxic leaves. Marsupial moles burrowed through the dry interior soil exactly as their unrelated placental counterparts excavate through soils elsewhere. Kangaroos perfected the bouncing gait that allows them to traverse vast distances at low energy cost, the legs functioning as living springs. Across every variety of marsupial, the offspring is born essentially as a foetus the size of a bean, blind and almost without limbs, and must crawl from the birth canal into the mother's pouch where it attaches to a teat and continues to develop for months. Every native Australian mammal you can name follows this pattern. The pouch is not a curiosity here. It constitutes the local default.

**Q1:** Which group of pouched Australian mammals diversified into every available ecological niche after placental mammals failed to arrive?
**A1:** marsupial

**Q2:** Which Australian marsupial ascended into the eucalyptus canopy and developed a metabolism specialised to digest its toxic leaves?
**A2:** koala

**Q3:** Which long-legged Australian marsupial perfected the bouncing gait, its limbs functioning as living springs that traverse vast distances at low energy cost?
**A3:** kangaroo

## 5.3  The Mammal That Lays Eggs

Australia hosts another evolutionary surprise even more disorienting than the marsupial. The platypus is a mammal that lays eggs rather than bearing live young, swims with webbed feet, possesses a bill resembling a duck's, a tail resembling a beaver's, and carries on the male's ankle a venomous spur capable of disabling a small dog. The first preserved specimen sent back to London by a colonial naturalist was widely assumed to be a hoax, with European biologists openly accusing the sender of stitching together body parts from unrelated animals to fabricate a fraud. Only when subsequent live specimens arrived did the scientific establishment accept that the creature was real. It belongs to a tiny order called the monotremes, the only surviving mammals that still reproduce by laying eggs, an evolutionary lineage that diverged before the marsupials and placentals had even separated from each other.

**Q1:** Which Australian mammal lays eggs, possesses a duck-like bill, a beaver-like tail, and a venomous spur on the male's ankle?
**A1:** platypus

**Q2:** What is the tiny mammalian order, including the platypus, defined by the surviving habit of reproduction through egg-laying?
**A2:** monotreme

**Q3:** What did European biologists initially suspect about the first platypus specimen sent to London by a colonial naturalist?
**A3:** hoax

## 5.4  The Songlines

Aboriginal Australians arrived on the continent during the Ice Age, crossing narrow stretches of ocean on bamboo rafts when the sea levels were considerably lower than they are today. They have therefore inhabited the same land continuously for longer than any other living culture on the planet. They developed no system of writing in the European sense, yet they devised something equally durable. The entire terrain of the continent, every water source, every dangerous animal, every safe ridge, was encoded into songs called songlines, which traverse the landscape and are transmitted orally from one generation to the next. A single song constitutes a complete navigational chart and ecological encyclopaedia simultaneously. To walk a songline is to follow a route an ancestor first sang into existence. The land and the song were never separated in Aboriginal cosmology, and to forget the song would be to lose the land itself.

**Q1:** Which oral system did Aboriginal Australians devise to encode the entire continental terrain, water sources, and dangers into songs transmitted across generations?
**A1:** songline

**Q2:** Which prehistoric global cooling period allowed Aboriginal Australians to cross narrow stretches of ocean and first reach the continent?
**A2:** Ice Age

**Q3:** Which native Australian population developed songlines and constitutes the oldest continuously surviving culture on the planet?
**A3:** Aboriginal Australians

## 5.5  Without a Compass

The Polynesian peoples of the Pacific possessed no compass, no chronometer, no astrolabe, and yet they navigated successfully across thousands of kilometres of open ocean to colonise nearly every habitable island in the largest body of water on the planet. They read the positions of stars by memory. They interpreted the colour and the temperature of ocean currents beneath their hands. They tracked the flight directions of seabirds to locate land below the horizon. They steered outrigger canoes from one island to the next across distances that European sailors, even centuries later with all their instruments, considered impossible to attempt. The Polynesians eventually reached Easter Island in the eastern Pacific and Aotearoa, modern New Zealand, in the southwest, the last major lands on Earth ever to be encountered by human beings. Every habitable rock between Hawaii and New Zealand carries the genetic legacy of those navigators.

**Q1:** Which Pacific people navigated across thousands of kilometres of open ocean by reading stars, currents, and seabird flight directions, without compass or chronometer?
**A1:** Polynesians

**Q2:** What kind of stabilising vessel did the Polynesians use to colonise nearly every habitable island in the Pacific?
**A2:** outrigger canoe

**Q3:** Which remote South Pacific island, modern New Zealand, was the last major land ever encountered by human navigators?
**A3:** Aotearoa

## 5.6  The Cleanest Ecological Suicide

On a particular remote Polynesian island, now called Easter Island, the original settlers gradually felled every tree they could find on their territory in order to roll the giant stone heads, called moai, across the volcanic terrain to ceremonial platforms along the coast. As the last tree fell, the islanders lost the ability to construct canoes. Without canoes, they lost the ability to fish offshore or to migrate elsewhere when famine arrived. Without the canopy of forest, the topsoil eroded into the sea. The chiefdoms that had once cooperated on the moai now began to wage war against each other, and the entire civilisation collapsed inside its own enclosed laboratory of resource exhaustion. Easter Island therefore constitutes the cleanest documented sample of an ecological suicide: a society that consumed the very environment which had been sustaining it, and could not stop in time to save itself.

**Q1:** Which giant stone heads did the original Easter Island settlers carve and roll across the volcanic terrain, felling every tree in the process?
**A1:** moai

**Q2:** Which watercraft did the Easter Islanders lose the ability to construct once every tree on the island had been felled?
**A2:** canoe

**Q3:** What kind of self-inflicted environmental collapse does Easter Island constitute the cleanest documented sample of?
**A3:** ecological suicide

## 5.7  The Open-Air Prison

Captain Cook charted the eastern coast of Australia, and the British Empire immediately recognised the value of the discovery: an entire continent on the far side of the Earth where convicts could be deposited and forgotten. Boatloads of prisoners began arriving in chains, and the colonies of New South Wales and Tasmania expanded across the next decades as open-air penal settlements. The European settlers, encountering an ecosystem they did not understand, classified the local marsupial wolf or thylacine as vermin and offered bounty payments for every carcass produced. The last thylacine died alone in a Hobart zoo cage, while a silent black-and-white film camera recorded it pacing its enclosure. That footage still survives in the Australian archives, and the species it preserves does not.

**Q1:** Which British naval captain charted the eastern coast of Australia and opened the continent to colonisation as an open-air penal settlement?
**A1:** Captain Cook

**Q2:** Which Tasmanian marsupial predator was classified as vermin by European settlers, hunted to extinction, and last documented pacing alone in a Hobart zoo cage?
**A2:** thylacine

**Q3:** What status did the British Empire assign to Australia, dispatching boatloads of convicts to its shores?
**A3:** penal colony

## 5.8  The Rabbit Plague

An English farmer released a small group of European rabbits onto his Australian estate so that he could shoot them for sport. Within decades the rabbit population, in the absence of any natural predator or local disease, exploded across the continent, consuming the vegetation of half a country and accelerating the erosion of every paddock they encountered. The Australian government constructed a rabbit-proof fence stretching for thousands of kilometres across the western half of the continent, which entirely failed to contain them. Decades later, the same authorities introduced cane toads from Hawaii to control insect pests in the sugarcane fields. The cane toad turned out to secrete a venom potent enough to terminate every native Australian predator that attempted to consume it, and the toads themselves dispersed unchecked. Australia is now, in places, the cautionary tale of every other continent's possible future.

**Q1:** Which European mammal, released onto an Australian estate for sport, exploded across the continent and consumed the vegetation of half a country?
**A1:** rabbit

**Q2:** What thousand-kilometre defensive structure did the Australian government erect to contain the rabbit invasion, ultimately failing to stop it?
**A2:** rabbit-proof fence

**Q3:** Which introduced Hawaiian amphibian secretes a venom potent enough to kill every native Australian predator that attempts to consume it?
**A3:** cane toad

## 5.9  The Reef That Bleaches

Along the northeastern coast of Australia stretches the Great Barrier Reef, the largest single living structure that the planet has ever produced. It is visible from low Earth orbit. It is constructed by the slow secretion of calcium carbonate by colonies of coral polyps cooperating with photosynthetic algae embedded in their tissues. When the surrounding sea temperature ascends only a few degrees above the long-term average, the corals expel their algal partners in stress, lose their colour, and starve to death within weeks. Vast stretches of the reef have already bleached. Meanwhile across the open Pacific, the low-lying nations of Tuvalu and the Marshall Islands are submerging year by year as the sea level rises, and their populations are preparing for a collective evacuation that has no precedent in human history. The Pacific is the front line of the climate crisis, and the reef is the first witness on it.

**Q1:** Which Australian marine structure, the largest single living formation on the planet, is constructed by colonies of coral polyps cooperating with photosynthetic algae?
**A1:** Great Barrier Reef

**Q2:** Which process do coral polyps undergo when sea temperatures ascend a few degrees above average, expelling their algal partners and starving to death?
**A2:** coral bleaching

**Q3:** Which low-lying Pacific nation is submerging year by year as sea levels rise, preparing its entire population for collective evacuation?
**A3:** Tuvalu
