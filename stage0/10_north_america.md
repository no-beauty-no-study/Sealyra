# Stage 0, Chapter 10: North America

## 10.1  The Bering Land Bridge

Across multiple ice ages the level of the world's oceans dropped sufficiently that a wide land corridor emerged between Siberia and Alaska, exposing dry tundra where the Bering Sea now reaches. Bands of Asian hunters tracking herds of mammoth and bison crossed eastward over the exposed bridge and within a few thousand years their descendants had populated every habitable region of the Americas from the Yukon to Tierra del Fuego. The same hunters appear to have annihilated, in combination with the warming climate, virtually every ice-age megafauna species they encountered. The mammoth, the giant ground sloth, the sabre-toothed cat, the giant short-faced bear, the American camel, and dozens of additional large mammals vanished from the fossil record within a few thousand years of human arrival. The continent that received the first Americans had already been reshaped by them before they themselves were established within it.

**Q1:** Which prehistoric exposed corridor between Siberia and Alaska did the first Americans cross to populate the entire western hemisphere?
**A1:** Bering land bridge

**Q2:** Which ice-age elephant relative, hunted across the new continent by the first Americans, vanished from the fossil record within a few thousand years of human arrival?
**A2:** mammoth

**Q3:** Which extinct American carnivore with elongated upper canines was annihilated alongside the mammoth by the first Americans and the warming climate?
**A3:** sabre-toothed cat

## 10.2  The Maya Pyramids

Deep in the Central American jungles a sophisticated indigenous civilisation arose without any contact with Eurasian culture. The Maya constructed stepped stone pyramids that ascended above the surrounding canopy, devised a writing system of glyphs that recorded astronomy and royal genealogies, and articulated a calendar so precise that the accumulated error after a millennium amounted to less than a single day. Their astronomers had calculated the orbit of Venus with sufficient accuracy to predict its appearances centuries in advance. Then, across roughly a single century, almost every major Maya city in the southern lowlands was simply abandoned. The population dispersed into smaller communities and the surrounding jungle reclaimed the platforms. Archaeologists continue to debate whether drought, internal warfare, ecological collapse, or some combination of these terminated the lowland Maya, but no single explanation has accounted for the full scale of the abandonment.

**Q1:** Which Central American indigenous civilisation constructed stepped stone pyramids above the jungle canopy and devised a writing system of glyphs?
**A1:** Maya

**Q2:** Which planet's orbit had Maya astronomers calculated with sufficient precision to predict its appearances centuries in advance?
**A2:** Venus

**Q3:** Which natural climatic catastrophe, debated among the candidate explanations for the lowland Maya disappearance, may have terminated the cities within a single century?
**A3:** drought

## 10.3  The City in the Lake

In the high valley of central Mexico the Aztec people constructed the floating city of Tenochtitlan, an island metropolis crisscrossed by canals, fed by chinampa agricultural rafts, and connected to the surrounding shore by raised causeways. At the height of the empire the city contained more inhabitants than any contemporary city in Europe. The Spanish conquistador Cortés arrived in Mexico with several hundred soldiers, formed alliances with subjugated indigenous nations who resented Aztec rule, and entered the city as a guest before seizing the emperor Moctezuma as a hostage. Yet the real conquest was achieved by a virus. Smallpox, accompanying the Spanish in the bodies of infected sailors, propagated through the indigenous population that possessed no immunity, exterminating roughly half of the inhabitants of Tenochtitlan including the new emperor within months. The Spanish then demolished the city itself and constructed modern Mexico City on top of its ruins.

**Q1:** Which floating Aztec city, an island metropolis fed by chinampa agricultural rafts and connected to the shore by causeways, was demolished after the Spanish conquest?
**A1:** Tenochtitlan

**Q2:** Which Spanish conquistador arrived in Mexico with several hundred soldiers, formed alliances with subjugated indigenous nations, and seized the Aztec emperor as a hostage?
**A2:** Cortés

**Q3:** Which virus, propagating through an indigenous population that possessed no immunity, achieved the actual conquest of the Aztec empire?
**A3:** smallpox

## 10.4  The Plains and the Confederacy

Across the immense grasslands of the North American interior, the Sioux, the Cheyenne, and several allied nations had developed an entire culture organised around the seasonal pursuit of the buffalo. The buffalo provided meat, hides for shelter, sinew for bowstrings, horn for tools, and even the dung that was burned for fuel on the treeless prairie. To the east, the Iroquois Confederacy united five separate nations under a single deliberative council that articulated a constitutional structure of separated powers, federated authority, and the consent of the governed. Benjamin Franklin and Thomas Jefferson studied this confederacy explicitly while drafting the American constitution, and acknowledged that the indigenous political design exceeded any European monarchy in sophistication. The American political experiment, in this sense, descended from indigenous political imagination as much as from European Enlightenment text.

**Q1:** Which great American grassland herbivore provided the Sioux and Cheyenne with meat, hides, sinew, horn, and even fuel from its dung?
**A1:** buffalo

**Q2:** Which federation of five Native American nations articulated a constitutional structure of separated powers that explicitly influenced the American constitution?
**A2:** Iroquois Confederacy

**Q3:** Which American founding father studied the Iroquois constitutional design while drafting the American constitution?
**A3:** Benjamin Franklin

## 10.5  The Plains and the Butterfly

The Rocky Mountains rise as a vast spine along the western interior of North America, intercepting the moisture-laden Pacific air and casting an immense rain shadow across the eastern Great Plains. Tens of millions of buffalo grazed across those plains before European arrival, the largest herd of large mammals on the planet outside the African savannah. Across the same grassland, every autumn, the monarch butterflies execute a migration that no individual butterfly can possibly complete within its own lifespan. They depart Canada and the northern United States, fly four thousand kilometres south to a small cluster of fir forests in central Mexico, and overwinter clustered together on the same individual trees their grandparents had departed from. Their migratory route is encoded entirely in their genes; no living butterfly has ever flown the full path before.

**Q1:** Which great mountain range rises along the western interior of North America, intercepting Pacific moisture and casting a rain shadow across the eastern plains?
**A1:** Rocky Mountains

**Q2:** Which orange and black North American insect migrates four thousand kilometres south to overwinter on the same fir trees its grandparents departed from?
**A2:** monarch butterfly

**Q3:** Which Mexican coniferous forest hosts the overwintering monarch butterflies after their continental migration each autumn?
**A3:** fir forest

## 10.6  The Lakes the Glaciers Left

Five enormous freshwater lakes occupy the basin between the United States and Canada, the remnants of the Pleistocene ice sheets that ground their depressions into the continental shield as they retreated. The Great Lakes contain more than a fifth of all the surface fresh water on the planet. The Niagara River connects Lake Erie to Lake Ontario, descending across a sheer cliff at Niagara Falls in a permanent vertical sheet of falling water. The cliff itself is gradually retreating upstream as the falling water erodes the rim, and at the present rate of erosion the falls will eventually consume Lake Erie entirely. The whole landscape from Quebec to Minnesota still bears the scars of glacial scouring: smoothed bedrock, mounded glacial till, scattered drumlins, and the long parallel scratches that the ice deposited as it withdrew.

**Q1:** Which group of five enormous North American freshwater lakes contains more than a fifth of all the surface fresh water on the planet?
**A1:** Great Lakes

**Q2:** Which famous river falls, gradually retreating upstream through erosion, will eventually consume Lake Erie at the present rate?
**A2:** Niagara Falls

**Q3:** Which prehistoric ice formations carved the Great Lakes basins into the continental shield as they retreated at the end of the last ice age?
**A3:** ice sheet

## 10.7  The Canyon, the Volcano, and the Glacier

The Colorado River has carved the Grand Canyon across millions of years into the elevated Colorado Plateau, eroding nearly two kilometres downward through layered sedimentary rock and exposing a geological column that spans almost two billion years of planetary history. The Yellowstone Plateau, further north, conceals beneath its surface a supervolcano whose magma chamber lies only a few kilometres below the ground; the entire region pulses with hot springs, geysers, and bubbling mud pools, and a sufficient future eruption would deposit ash across the whole continent. Northward still, the glaciers of Alaska descend from the Coast Mountains toward the Pacific, calving icebergs directly into the cold fjords where humpback whales and orcas hunt for salmon and seal. North America preserves geological extremes that no other continent quite matches.

**Q1:** Which immense canyon, carved by the Colorado River into the Colorado Plateau, exposes a geological column spanning almost two billion years?
**A1:** Grand Canyon

**Q2:** Which American national park conceals beneath its surface a supervolcano whose future eruption would deposit ash across the whole continent?
**A2:** Yellowstone

**Q3:** Which periodic Yellowstone hot-water eruption, fed by the magma chamber below, propels boiling water vertically into the air at predictable intervals?
**A3:** geyser

## 10.8  The Mayflower Winter

A small group of English religious dissenters called Pilgrims sailed westward across the Atlantic on the Mayflower seeking the freedom to practise their reformed Protestant faith without persecution. Their intended destination had been the colony of Virginia, but storms drove them northward and they made landfall instead on the coast of modern Massachusetts. The first winter eradicated nearly half of the small community through cold, hunger, and disease. The survivors were tutored through the following spring by the local Wampanoag people, who taught them which crops could be cultivated in the New England soil and how to harvest the local turkey, deer, and shellfish. At the autumn harvest the two communities convened a shared feast to acknowledge a year of survival, and the meal that resulted has been ritualised as the American national holiday of Thanksgiving.

**Q1:** Which English religious dissenters sailed westward on the Mayflower seeking the freedom to practise their reformed Protestant faith without persecution?
**A1:** Pilgrims

**Q2:** Which indigenous people tutored the Pilgrim survivors through the following spring, teaching them which crops could be cultivated and how to harvest local food?
**A2:** Wampanoag

**Q3:** Which American national holiday ritualises the shared autumn harvest feast convened by the Pilgrims and the Wampanoag after a year of survival?
**A3:** Thanksgiving

## 10.9  The Kite and the Tea

Benjamin Franklin flew a kite into a thunderstorm with a key attached to the string, demonstrating that lightning consists of the same electrical current that could be generated in a Leyden jar in his Philadelphia laboratory, and devised the lightning rod as a domestic application of his finding. A few decades later, when the British Parliament imposed yet another tax on the American colonies without permitting them representation in the parliament that taxed them, Bostonian patriots disguised as Mohawk warriors boarded three British ships in the harbour and dumped the entire cargo of taxed tea into the sea. The resulting confrontation escalated into the American Revolutionary War, and the lawyer Thomas Jefferson drafted the Declaration of Independence whose opening clause asserted that all men are created equal, although Jefferson himself owned several hundred enslaved Africans for the entirety of his life.

**Q1:** Which American polymath flew a kite into a thunderstorm with a key on the string, demonstrating that lightning is electrical current and devising the lightning rod?
**A1:** Benjamin Franklin

**Q2:** Which Massachusetts protest dumped three shiploads of taxed British tea into the harbour, igniting the American Revolutionary War?
**A2:** Boston Tea Party

**Q3:** Which American founding document, drafted by Jefferson, asserted that all men are created equal despite Jefferson's own ownership of several hundred enslaved Africans?
**A3:** Declaration of Independence

## 10.10  The Gold and the Tears

Word that gold had been discovered in California provoked hundreds of thousands of prospectors to migrate westward by wagon train and clipper ship, transforming the small port of San Francisco into an instant metropolis. To connect the new western settlements with the eastern population centres the United States constructed the transcontinental railroad, blasting tunnels through the Sierra Nevada with the labour of Irish immigrants and imported Chinese workers, many of whom died on the explosive charges. To clear the territory for white settlement, the Cherokee, the Choctaw, the Creek, and the Seminole were forcibly removed from their ancestral lands in the southeastern United States and marched westward at gunpoint to designated reservations in Oklahoma. A quarter of the marchers died from exposure, hunger, and disease along the way. The route they were forced to traverse is now called the Trail of Tears.

**Q1:** Which mid-nineteenth-century mineral discovery in California provoked hundreds of thousands of prospectors to migrate westward and transformed San Francisco into an instant metropolis?
**A1:** California Gold Rush

**Q2:** Which east-west infrastructure project blasted tunnels through the Sierra Nevada with Irish and Chinese labour, connecting the new western settlements with the east?
**A2:** transcontinental railroad

**Q3:** Which forced relocation route, on which a quarter of the marchers died from exposure and disease, marched the Cherokee and other southeastern nations to Oklahoma reservations?
**A3:** Trail of Tears

## 10.11  The War Over Slavery

The economic basis of the southern United States had become the cotton plantation, whose operation absolutely required the unpaid labour of several million enslaved Africans. The northern states, having industrialised earlier, no longer relied on slavery and increasingly opposed it on moral grounds. The election of the moderate antislavery lawyer Abraham Lincoln to the presidency provoked the southern states to secede and the resulting Civil War terminated more American lives than every subsequent American war combined. Lincoln signed the Emancipation Proclamation and was assassinated in a Washington theatre by a southern sympathiser just as the war was ending. Legal abolition did not generate equality. Black Americans endured another full century of segregation, lynching, and disenfranchisement before the civil rights activist Martin Luther King delivered his I Have a Dream speech and Congress finally enforced racial equality through federal legislation.

**Q1:** Which American conflict terminated more American lives than every subsequent American war combined, originating in the southern states' secession to preserve slavery?
**A1:** Civil War

**Q2:** Which American president signed the Emancipation Proclamation and was assassinated in a Washington theatre just as the war was ending?
**A2:** Abraham Lincoln

**Q3:** Which American civil rights activist delivered the I Have a Dream speech, provoking federal legislation to enforce racial equality?
**A3:** Martin Luther King

## 10.12  The Music Out of the Field

The work songs of enslaved Africans labouring in cotton fields, the call-and-response hollers that synchronised the rhythm of group labour, and the spirituals adapted from biblical text, transformed across generations into the secular music known as the blues. As Black migrants moved north into Chicago and New York during the twentieth century, the blues collided with European harmonic theory and improvisational virtuosity to generate jazz, the first uniquely American art form. Soul music and rhythm and blues evolved out of the gospel choir. The white singer Elvis Presley adapted Black rhythm and blues into the rock and roll that made an entire generation of white American teenagers lose their senses for Black-originated music. Bronx Black teenagers eventually layered spoken rhyme over looped instrumental samples and generated hip-hop, the dominant global popular music of the present moment. Every contemporary popular song descends from a field holler.

**Q1:** Which African American musical tradition, derived from cotton-field work songs, call-and-response hollers, and adapted spirituals, transformed across generations into a foundational genre?
**A1:** blues

**Q2:** Which uniquely American musical art form emerged when Black blues migrants moved north and collided with European harmonic theory and improvisational virtuosity?
**A2:** jazz

**Q3:** Which contemporary global popular music emerged when Bronx Black teenagers layered spoken rhyme over looped instrumental samples?
**A3:** hip-hop

## 10.13  The Light and the Spark

The American inventor Edison opened a research laboratory in New Jersey and generated the first commercially viable incandescent light bulb, the phonograph, and the motion picture camera, eventually accumulating over a thousand patents. He insisted that direct electrical current was the only safe form of household electricity and toured the country electrocuting stray dogs and even an elephant to demonstrate the supposed dangers of his rival's system. His Serbian-born former assistant Nikola Tesla, working with the manufacturer Westinghouse, advocated alternating current, whose lower transmission losses across long distances permitted electricity to be distributed economically from a single generating station to an entire city. The physics ultimately favoured Tesla and the American electrical grid was constructed on alternating current. Tesla himself, despite an immense list of inventions, died in poverty in a small New York hotel, while Edison left a vast industrial empire and his name on every major American utility.

**Q1:** Which American inventor accumulated over a thousand patents, generating the first commercially viable light bulb, the phonograph, and the motion picture camera?
**A1:** Edison

**Q2:** Which Serbian-born inventor advocated alternating current against Edison's direct current, winning the engineering debate but dying in poverty in a small New York hotel?
**A2:** Tesla

**Q3:** Which form of electrical current ultimately constituted the basis of the American electrical grid because of its lower transmission losses across distance?
**A3:** alternating current

## 10.14  The Telephone, the Aeroplane, the Assembly Line

The Scottish-American Alexander Graham Bell, attempting to design a hearing aid for his deaf mother, accidentally invented the telephone instead and demonstrated for the first time that the human voice could be transmitted across an electrical wire. The Wright brothers, who repaired bicycles in Ohio for a living, conducted patient aerodynamic experiments on the dunes of North Carolina until they achieved the first sustained powered flight in a wooden glider whose wings they had warped through cable-and-pulley control. Henry Ford devised the moving automobile assembly line in Detroit, dropping the price of the Model T to a level that ordinary American workers could afford, and effectively converted the automobile from an aristocratic toy into the everyday means of transport that has restructured every American city built afterward.

**Q1:** Which Scottish-American inventor, attempting to design a hearing aid for his deaf mother, accidentally invented the telephone instead?
**A1:** Alexander Graham Bell

**Q2:** Which two Ohio bicycle-repairing brothers achieved the first sustained powered flight in a wooden glider on the dunes of North Carolina?
**A2:** Wright brothers

**Q3:** Which American industrialist devised the moving automobile assembly line in Detroit, converting the automobile from an aristocratic toy into everyday transport?
**A3:** Henry Ford

## 10.15  The Tycoon and the Library

John D. Rockefeller began with a single Ohio refinery and across his career consolidated ninety per cent of American oil refining into a single corporation called Standard Oil, accumulating personal wealth that has never been exceeded by any subsequent American. Andrew Carnegie migrated as a child from Scotland to the Pittsburgh steel mills, started as a textile factory child labourer, and built the American steel industry into a vertically integrated empire he sold for hundreds of millions of dollars at retirement. Both men suppressed worker strikes with private armies during their accumulation phase. Yet both ultimately donated the majority of their immense fortunes to founding libraries, universities, museums, and concert halls, and roughly half of the major American cultural and educational institutions of the present day exist because of the redistribution of their gilded-age accumulations.

**Q1:** Which American oil tycoon consolidated ninety per cent of American oil refining into Standard Oil, accumulating wealth that has never been exceeded by any subsequent American?

**Q2:** Which Scottish-born American industrialist built the American steel industry into a vertically integrated empire and sold it for hundreds of millions of dollars at retirement?
**A2:** Carnegie

## 10.16  The Crash and the War

The collapse of the New York stock market initiated a decade of unemployment, bank failures, and agricultural devastation now remembered as the Great Depression. The newly elected president Franklin Roosevelt launched a programme called the New Deal in which the federal government directly employed millions of workers on dams, parks, post offices, and conservation projects, partially restoring economic activity. The Japanese surprise attack on the American naval base at Pearl Harbor dragged the United States into the Second World War, after which Roosevelt converted the entire American industrial capacity to military production: automobile factories assembled tanks, cosmetic factories filled artillery shells, and American industrial output eventually constituted the decisive factor in the Allied victory.

**Q1:** Which decade-long American economic catastrophe, initiated by the collapse of the New York stock market, generated unemployment, bank failures, and agricultural devastation?
**A1:** Great Depression

**Q2:** Which Roosevelt programme directly employed millions of American workers on dams, parks, post offices, and conservation projects, partially restoring economic activity?
**A2:** New Deal

**Q3:** Which Japanese surprise attack on an American naval base dragged the United States into the Second World War?
**A3:** Pearl Harbor

## 10.17  The Bomb

Albert Einstein, having fled Nazi Germany for the institute at Princeton, wrote to President Roosevelt warning that German physicists might exploit nuclear fission to construct a weapon of unprecedented destructive capacity. The American Manhattan Project recruited several thousand physicists, engineers, and mathematicians to a secret laboratory in the New Mexico desert, where, across a few years of feverish work, they generated the first functional nuclear weapon. The bombs incinerated Hiroshima and Nagasaki within days of each other, instantly killing approximately a hundred thousand people each and terminating Japanese resistance, ending the war. The shadow the Manhattan Project cast across the subsequent half-century, however, has never lifted. Every superpower confrontation since has been conducted in the perpetual awareness that nuclear weapons remained available to escalate the conflict beyond any limit.

**Q1:** Which Princeton-based physicist warned President Roosevelt that German physicists might exploit nuclear fission to construct a weapon of unprecedented destructive capacity?
**A1:** Einstein

**Q2:** Which American secret programme recruited several thousand physicists to a New Mexico laboratory to generate the first functional nuclear weapon?
**A2:** Manhattan Project

**Q3:** Which two Japanese cities were incinerated by American nuclear weapons within days of each other, terminating Japanese resistance and ending the Second World War?
**A3:** Hiroshima and Nagasaki

## 10.18  The Race to the Moon

When the Soviet Union launched the first artificial satellite into low Earth orbit and the first cosmonaut into space, the United States, perceiving itself overtaken, established the National Aeronautics and Space Administration and committed to landing astronauts on the surface of the Moon. The Apollo programme accumulated roughly a quarter of a million workers and immense federal funding; on the sixth attempt, Apollo Eleven landed Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin on the lunar Sea of Tranquillity while a global audience watched the broadcast. NASA also dispatched the Voyager probes outward past Jupiter and Saturn and out of the solar system entirely. Each probe carries a gold-plated phonograph record encoded with greetings in fifty-five human languages and several samples of music, addressed to any civilisation that might eventually intercept it across the interstellar dark.

**Q1:** Which American space agency was established in response to the Soviet launch of the first satellite, committing to land astronauts on the Moon?
**A1:** NASA

**Q2:** Which American programme accumulated a quarter of a million workers and landed Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin on the lunar Sea of Tranquillity?
**A2:** Apollo

**Q3:** Which American interstellar probes carry gold-plated phonograph records encoded with greetings in fifty-five human languages, addressed to any civilisation that might intercept them?
**A3:** Voyager

## 10.19  The Mind in the Laboratory

Twentieth-century American psychology laboratories conducted a series of experiments that progressively dismantled the optimistic conception of the human mind. Skinner trained pigeons to perform sequential behaviours through reinforcement schedules, demonstrating that complex action could be shaped entirely from external incentives. Milgram instructed ordinary participants to administer escalating electrical shocks to another person on the instruction of a researcher in a white coat; most participants complied. Zimbardo divided Stanford undergraduates into mock prisoners and mock guards, and within a week the guards had begun systematically abusing the prisoners until the experiment was forcibly terminated. The marshmallow study tracked children who could delay gratification against those who could not, finding measurable life-outcome differences decades later. Kahneman's prospect theory eventually demonstrated that human decision-making under uncertainty is systematically irrational in predictable ways, and won him a Nobel in economics.

**Q1:** Which American behaviourist trained pigeons to perform sequential behaviours through reinforcement schedules, demonstrating that complex action could be shaped from external incentives?
**A1:** Skinner

**Q2:** Which American social psychologist instructed ordinary participants to administer escalating electrical shocks under researcher authority, demonstrating disturbing rates of compliance?
**A2:** Milgram

**Q3:** Which Stanford undergraduate study divided participants into mock prisoners and mock guards, terminated forcibly within a week after the guards began systematically abusing the prisoners?
**A3:** Stanford prison experiment

## 10.20  The Code of Life

The American biologist Watson and the British physicist Crick, working at Cambridge with crucial X-ray crystallographic data produced by Rosalind Franklin, deduced that the molecule of heredity, DNA, takes the form of a double helix, two intertwined strands of complementary base pairs. Across the following half-century, American biomedical research generated the polio vaccine developed by Salk that virtually eliminated the disease, the immortal HeLa cell line cultured from a Black patient's cancer without her consent, the complete sequencing of the human genome, and the CRISPR gene-editing technique that has begun to enable the deliberate modification of inherited traits. The combination of these developments has, within a single century, transformed humans from creatures who passively suffered their biological inheritance into creatures who actively edit it.

**Q1:** Which molecular structure did Watson, Crick, and Franklin's data deduce, consisting of two intertwined strands of complementary base pairs that encode heredity?
**A1:** DNA double helix

**Q2:** Which American vaccine, developed by Salk, virtually eliminated the disease that had paralysed tens of thousands of American children every year?
**A2:** polio vaccine

**Q3:** Which gene-editing technique has begun to enable the deliberate modification of inherited traits in living organisms?
**A3:** CRISPR

## 10.21  The Garage

A region of small fruit orchards south of San Francisco transformed across a few decades into the technological centre of the planet. Several young engineers assembled the first commercially viable personal computers from kits in their garages: Jobs and Wozniak founded Apple, while Gates and Allen built the operating system that ran on a competing IBM machine. The American Defense Department had connected a small network of university computers across the country into ARPANET, the original ancestor of the global internet, and a British computer scientist working at the European nuclear research facility eventually devised the World Wide Web protocol that permitted ordinary citizens to navigate the network through hyperlinks. The cumulative effect across roughly two decades was to migrate computing from government laboratories into the pockets of every literate human being on the planet.

**Q1:** Which American technology region, originally a landscape of fruit orchards south of San Francisco, transformed across a few decades into the technological centre of the planet?
**A1:** Silicon Valley

**Q2:** Which two American engineers assembled the first commercially viable personal computers in a garage and founded Apple?
**A2:** Jobs and Wozniak

**Q3:** Which original American military computer network connected university computers across the country and became the ancestor of the global internet?
**A3:** ARPANET

## 10.22  The Pocket Computer

Steve Jobs returned to Apple after a decade of exile and presented the iPhone, integrating a touch-sensitive screen, a personal computer, a phone, a camera, and a portable internet terminal into a single device that fits inside an ordinary pocket. Within a decade nearly every adult on the planet was conducting daily life through a similar device. Google indexed the entire navigable Web and converted general search into a single corporate utility. Facebook accumulated the social relationships of billions of users into a single database. American research laboratories then trained large language models on virtually the entire accessible text of the internet, generating systems capable of producing fluent prose, answering questions, and assisting with intellectual work at scale. OpenAI's ChatGPT and Anthropic's Claude eventually presented these capabilities to the general public, and the same companies are now openly debating whether the trajectory of artificial intelligence has begun to exceed human design.

## 10.23  The Silent Spring

The marine biologist Rachel Carson published a book demonstrating that the pesticide DDT, sprayed liberally on American agricultural land across the post-war decades, was accumulating in the tissues of insectivorous birds and thinning the shells of their eggs to the point that virtually no chicks were hatching. The book ignited the modern environmental movement, generated the United States Environmental Protection Agency, and prompted the federal prohibition of DDT. Decades later, ecologists reintroduced the grey wolf into Yellowstone National Park, where the absence of large predators had permitted elk populations to overgraze every accessible riverbank. The wolves restored the trophic balance of the entire ecosystem, the streams recovered their vegetation, and the resident beaver populations returned. Meanwhile honeybees have been disappearing from American commercial pollination operations through a combination of pesticide exposure and parasitic mite infestation, threatening the entire structure of American agriculture.

**Q1:** Which American marine biologist published the book demonstrating that DDT was accumulating in bird tissue and thinning their eggshells, igniting the modern environmental movement?
**A1:** Rachel Carson

**Q2:** Which large predator was reintroduced into Yellowstone, restoring the trophic balance of the entire ecosystem as the streams recovered their vegetation?
**A2:** grey wolf

**Q3:** Which essential American pollinator has been disappearing from commercial operations through pesticide exposure and parasitic mite infestation, threatening the entire agricultural structure?
**A3:** honeybee

## 10.24  The Splatter and the Soup Can

After the Second World War the centre of the visual art world migrated from Paris to New York. Jackson Pollock laid an immense canvas on his studio floor and dripped industrial enamel paint from sticks and brushes in elaborate gestural patterns, generating abstract expressionism. Mark Rothko produced enormous canvases of stacked soft-edged coloured rectangles that overwhelmed the viewer through pure chromatic vibration. Andy Warhol abandoned the entire premise of unique art by silk-screening Campbell soup cans, Marilyn Monroe headshots, and other commercial imagery into dozens of identical reproductions, founding pop art and declaring the boundary between fine art and commerce obsolete. The young Black painter Basquiat emerged from New York graffiti subculture, conducted a brief intense career, and overdosed at twenty-seven. The Museum of Modern Art in New York became the global capital of contemporary art curation.

## 10.25  Hollywood

A handful of Jewish immigrant entrepreneurs migrated west from New York to the small Los Angeles suburb of Hollywood and established the first film studios there. Across the twentieth century these studios consolidated the entire industrial pipeline from script through production through global distribution into the most dominant cultural export the planet has ever seen. The Disney animation studio constructed its first feature with Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs, then expanded into theme parks, television networks, and merchandise empires until Disney effectively owned a substantial proportion of childhood worldwide. The Broadway theatre district in midtown Manhattan generated musicals fusing song, dialogue, choreography, and elaborate stage production: Phantom of the Opera, The Lion King, Hamilton. American film and popular music have so saturated global culture that adolescents in countries the studios have never visited can sing along with every chorus.

**Q1:** Which Los Angeles suburb did Jewish immigrant entrepreneurs establish in the early twentieth century as the global film industry capital?
**A1:** Hollywood

**Q2:** Which American animation studio constructed its first feature with Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs and expanded into the dominant childhood entertainment empire on the planet?
**A2:** Disney

**Q3:** Which midtown Manhattan theatre district generates musicals fusing song, dialogue, choreography, and elaborate stage production?

## 10.26  The Building Reaches Up

After the Chicago Fire, engineers invented the steel-skeleton load-bearing structure, which permitted buildings to ascend dozens of storeys above the ground without requiring stone walls thick enough to support their own weight, and the Manhattan skyline began to climb. The American architect Frank Lloyd Wright moved in the opposite direction, designing the residence Fallingwater directly above a waterfall in the Pennsylvania forest, its horizontal concrete terraces extending out over the falling water like rock outcrops generated by the landscape itself. The Canadian-born architect Frank Gehry departed from straight lines entirely, designing the Guggenheim Museum in Bilbao as a billowing skin of crumpled titanium that catches the surrounding light in unpredictable directions, inaugurating the deconstructivist tendency that has dominated experimental architecture across the present century.

## 10.27  The University

Eight private universities along the northeastern American coast collectively form what is now known as the Ivy League, distinguished by enormous accumulated endowments, selective admissions, and tenured faculty who can pursue unpopular research without dismissal. The Massachusetts Institute of Technology and Stanford University, the principal engineering schools of the country, have together graduated most of the founders of the Silicon Valley technology industry. The Scholastic Aptitude Test became the standardised gateway through which American adolescents are sorted into the available universities, and international students have constituted an increasingly large fraction of American doctoral programmes for decades, particularly in the sciences. Affirmative action policies attempting to increase the representation of historically underrepresented groups have been challenged in federal court repeatedly and most recently overturned, generating an unresolved debate about which forms of preference are constitutionally acceptable in admissions.

**Q1:** Which eight private universities along the northeastern American coast collectively form a league distinguished by accumulated endowments and selective admissions?
**A1:** Ivy League

**Q2:** Which two principal American engineering universities have together graduated most of the founders of the Silicon Valley technology industry?
**A2:** MIT and Stanford

**Q3:** Which standardised American admissions test became the gateway through which adolescents are sorted into the available universities?
**A3:** SAT

## 10.28  The Long Vote

American women acquired the legal right to vote only after a prolonged campaign of demonstrations, hunger strikes, and imprisonment by suffragettes, who were granted the franchise nationally through the Nineteenth Amendment. The Supreme Court in Roe versus Wade subsequently established a constitutional right to abortion, which the same court overturned half a century later in a decision restoring authority over reproductive medicine to the individual states. Title Nine of the Education Amendments required federally funded American schools to provide equal opportunity to female students, generating the explosion of women's athletic participation that has since produced multiple Olympic dynasties. The #MeToo movement, ignited by allegations against the film producer Harvey Weinstein, eventually unseated dozens of powerful men in entertainment, journalism, finance, and government on credible accusations of sexual assault and harassment.

**Q1:** Which American constitutional amendment finally granted women the legal right to vote nationally after a prolonged campaign of demonstrations and imprisonment?
**A1:** Nineteenth Amendment

**Q2:** Which American Supreme Court decision originally established a constitutional right to abortion, before being overturned half a century later?
**A2:** Roe v Wade

**Q3:** Which contemporary American movement, ignited by allegations against the film producer Harvey Weinstein, unseated dozens of powerful men on credible accusations of sexual assault?
**A3:** #MeToo

## 10.29  The Long Out

A confrontation between police and the patrons of a Manhattan bar called the Stonewall Inn precipitated several nights of street protest by the lesbian, gay, bisexual, and transgender community, and inaugurated the modern LGBTQ rights movement. Across the subsequent half-century the movement achieved the legalisation of same-sex marriage by the United States Supreme Court, the codification of workplace protection, and the increasing recognition of transgender rights, although the latter remains politically contested in many states. The Black Lives Matter movement emerged in response to the deaths of unarmed Black Americans in police custody, with the recorded killing of George Floyd in Minneapolis igniting the largest civil rights protests in American history. The Standing Rock Sioux Tribe meanwhile organised an extended encampment to oppose the construction of a petroleum pipeline beneath their reservation's water supply, generating a global indigenous solidarity movement.

**Q1:** Which Manhattan bar confrontation precipitated several nights of street protest and inaugurated the modern LGBTQ rights movement?
**A1:** Stonewall

**Q2:** Which American civil rights movement emerged in response to the deaths of unarmed Black Americans in police custody and ignited the largest protests in American history?
**A2:** Black Lives Matter

**Q3:** Which Sioux tribe organised an extended encampment to oppose the construction of a petroleum pipeline beneath their reservation's water supply?
**A3:** Standing Rock

## 10.30  The Bill Arrives

A coordinated attack by hijacked passenger aircraft demolished the World Trade Center towers in Manhattan and damaged the Pentagon in Washington, killing approximately three thousand civilians. The United States invaded Afghanistan and Iraq in response, sustained two decades of inconclusive Middle Eastern warfare, and eventually withdrew with virtually nothing accomplished. Climate change began meanwhile to manifest directly on the American landscape. California wildfires consumed entire towns. Hurricane Katrina demolished much of New Orleans, exposing the racial inequality of American disaster response. Arctic sea ice retreated dramatically year by year, polar bear populations dwindled, and indigenous Alaskan villages were forced to relocate inland as their coastlines eroded. The American continent is now absorbing the cumulative bill for several centuries of industrial extraction and colonial expansion, and the bill has only just begun to be presented.

**Q1:** Which coordinated attack by hijacked aircraft demolished the World Trade Center towers in Manhattan, killing approximately three thousand civilians?
**A1:** 9/11

**Q2:** Which 2005 hurricane demolished much of New Orleans, exposing the racial inequality of American disaster response?
**A2:** Hurricane Katrina

**Q3:** Which retreating Arctic ice formation, dwindling year by year, is forcing indigenous Alaskan villages to relocate inland as their coastlines erode?
**A3:** sea ice
