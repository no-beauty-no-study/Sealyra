# Stage 0, Chapter 7: South America

---

## 7.1  The Andes from the Sea

Across millions of years the Pacific plate has slowly subducted beneath the South American continent, lifting the entire western edge upward into a vast mountain spine we now designate the Andes. The thrust of that subduction has been so persistent that fossilised marine shells are still being discovered on the summit ridges of the Andes today, evidence that the rock now standing five thousand metres above the sea once lay submerged beneath it. The same subduction has triggered a chain of active volcanoes along the western coast and induced the frequent earthquakes that punctuate Chilean and Peruvian history, a geological reminder that the continent itself is still being assembled.

**Q1:** Which great mountain range was lifted across the western edge of South America by the persistent subduction of the Pacific plate?
**A1:** Andes

**Q2:** What ancient fossils, still discovered on Andean summit ridges, demonstrate that the rock once lay submerged beneath the sea?
**A2:** marine shells

**Q3:** What seismic activity along the western South American coast is induced by the same subduction that constructed the Andes?
**A3:** earthquake

## 7.2  Darwin and the Finches

The young naturalist Charles Darwin sailed aboard a British vessel called the Beagle that paused at the Galápagos Islands, a chain of volcanic outcrops drifting in the eastern Pacific. On every island Darwin encountered a slightly different variety of small finch, and the beaks of each variety differed in measurable ways: thick and powerful where the food consisted of seeds, narrow and pointed where the food consisted of insects. Darwin realised that a single ancestral species had diversified gradually into multiple distinct forms by adapting to the particular conditions of each island. He had glimpsed the mechanism that constitutes the foundation of modern biology. Knowing how thoroughly his society would resist the implication that humans had emerged from the same mechanism, he suppressed the manuscript for more than two decades before he finally dared to publish On the Origin of Species.

**Q1:** Which young British naturalist observed beak variation among finches on the Galápagos and realised that species could diversify gradually from a single ancestor?
**A1:** Charles Darwin

**Q2:** Which Pacific archipelago, where Darwin made his crucial observations, drifts off the western coast of Ecuador?
**A2:** Galápagos

**Q3:** Which biological book, suppressed for two decades before publication, articulated the mechanism by which species diversify through adaptation?
**A3:** On the Origin of Species

## 7.3  The Amazon Drowns the Sea

The Amazon River begins as a small glacial trickle near the summit of the Andes and traverses the entire South American continent from west to east, discharging finally into the Atlantic Ocean at a mouth so wide you cannot see the opposite shore from the middle of the channel. The Amazon delivers a volume of fresh water sufficient to push the saltwater of the ocean back a hundred kilometres offshore, and satellites in orbit can observe the colour difference between the freshwater plume and the surrounding sea, a distinct layered pattern visible from space. The forest that the Amazon sustains constitutes the largest single biome on the planet, accommodating more species per square kilometre than any other ecosystem ever generated by evolution.

**Q1:** Which South American river traverses the continent from the Andean glaciers to the Atlantic, delivering enough fresh water to push the ocean back a hundred kilometres offshore?
**A1:** Amazon

**Q2:** Which forest ecosystem, sustained by the Amazon, constitutes the single largest biome on the planet?
**A2:** rainforest

**Q3:** What is the geographical name for the wide outflow at the end of a great river where it meets the sea?
**A3:** mouth

## 7.4  The Forest of Bright Warnings

The Amazon rainforest is the most thoroughly disguised landscape on the planet. Sloths cultivate algae on their own fur in order to merge perfectly with the surrounding foliage. The mimic octopus impersonates rocks, leaves, and even the local poisonous fish in order to evade predators. The poison dart frog, by contrast, embraces the opposite strategy entirely: it advertises itself in vivid red, yellow, and blue patterns precisely because the venom on its skin can terminate a human heart with a single drop. Indigenous hunters extract the venom by simply wiping a blowdart tip across the frog's skin. Within moments any monkey or bird struck by the dart drops paralysed from the canopy. Every brightly coloured creature in the rainforest is sending you the same one-sentence message, which is: do not eat me, I am ready.

**Q1:** Which Amazon mammal cultivates algae on its own fur in order to merge perfectly with the surrounding foliage?
**A1:** sloth

**Q2:** Which Amazonian amphibian advertises itself in vivid colour because the venom on its skin can terminate a human heart with a single drop?
**A2:** poison dart frog

**Q3:** Which weapon do indigenous Amazon hunters tip with frog venom to paralyse monkeys and birds from below the canopy?
**A3:** blowdart

## 7.5  Patagonia and the Ice-Age Giants

The southernmost extremity of South America is Patagonia, a vast wind-scoured wasteland in which the constant gale from the Pacific bends the few surviving trees into permanent leeward angles. Glaciers descend from the surrounding mountains and crawl slowly toward the Atlantic, where their leading edges occasionally fragment into immense calving icebergs that plunge into the fjords with a sound like distant artillery. Darwin during his Beagle voyage excavated the fossilised bones of an extinct giant ground sloth in the soil of Patagonia, proving that South America had once supported its own community of ice-age megafauna. Like the woolly mammoth in North America, these South American giants persisted until human hunters arrived from the north, and then they vanished from the geological record within a few centuries.

**Q1:** Which southernmost extremity of South America is a vast wind-scoured wasteland whose constant gale bends every surviving tree into a permanent leeward angle?
**A1:** Patagonia

**Q2:** What extinct giant Patagonian mammal, whose fossil Darwin excavated during the Beagle voyage, persisted in South America until human hunters arrived?
**A2:** giant ground sloth

**Q3:** What deep coastal inlet, carved by descending glaciers, receives the calving icebergs that fragment from their leading edges?
**A3:** fjord

## 7.6  The Driest Place on Earth

The Atacama Desert occupies a narrow coastal strip along the western edge of South America, where the cold Humboldt current offshore cools the air and prevents any moisture from condensing into rain. Several valleys within the Atacama have not recorded a single drop of precipitation in centuries. NASA tests its Mars rovers in the Atacama because the local environment so closely resembles the surface of Mars that no other terrestrial site approximates the comparison. For the same reason, astronomers have constructed the largest array of radio telescopes on the planet here: the dry cloudless air and the absence of artificial light permit observations of the universe that no other location on Earth can match. The very absence that makes the Atacama uninhabitable is what renders it scientifically irreplaceable.

**Q1:** Which South American coastal desert, the driest place on Earth, contains valleys that have not recorded a drop of precipitation in centuries?
**A1:** Atacama

**Q2:** Which cold offshore ocean current cools the air over the Atacama and prevents any moisture from condensing into rain?
**A2:** Humboldt current

**Q3:** What kind of telescope array, the largest on the planet, has been constructed in the Atacama to exploit its dry cloudless skies?
**A3:** radio telescope

## 7.7  The Empire Without Writing

The Inca empire occupied the entire spine of the Andes from modern Colombia to Chile without ever inventing a system of writing or constructing a single wheeled vehicle. Yet across that vast vertical territory the Incas maintained a network of mountain paths so extensive that messages could traverse from one end of the empire to the other in a matter of days. Information was encoded into knotted cords called quipus, which trained record-keepers could interpret with precision, and the messages themselves were carried by relay runners called chaski who sprinted between station and station along the high passes. A single instruction issued from the capital could descend across snow ridges and arrive at the coast before any horse-mounted European messenger could have traversed even a fraction of the same distance.

**Q1:** Which South American empire occupied the entire spine of the Andes without ever inventing writing or constructing a single wheeled vehicle?
**A1:** Inca

**Q2:** Which knotted cord system did the Incas use to encode information that trained record-keepers could interpret with precision?
**A2:** quipu

**Q3:** What name did the Inca relay runners carry, who sprinted between high stations to transmit messages across the empire?
**A3:** chaski

## 7.8  Machu Picchu Hidden in the Mist

High along a remote Andean ridge the Incas constructed the citadel now called Machu Picchu. The masons cut every block of granite with such precision that the resulting walls accommodate no gap wide enough to insert the blade of a knife, and no mortar of any kind was applied between the stones. The walls have nevertheless persisted intact through centuries of earthquakes that destroyed every Spanish church in the same region. After the empire collapsed, the citadel was simply abandoned and overgrown by the surrounding jungle, vanishing from outside knowledge for nearly four hundred years until an American historian stumbled into it almost by accident while searching for a different ruin altogether. The ridge had concealed the city in the clouds for so long that the Spanish conquistadors never even suspected it existed.

**Q1:** Which Andean citadel did the Incas construct on a high remote ridge using granite blocks cut so precisely that no mortar was required between them?
**A1:** Machu Picchu

**Q2:** What kind of binding material did Inca masons deliberately omit between the granite blocks of Machu Picchu, which has nevertheless persisted through centuries of earthquakes?
**A2:** mortar

**Q3:** Which Spanish invaders never suspected the existence of Machu Picchu because the surrounding ridge concealed it in the clouds?
**A3:** conquistadors

## 7.9  The Microbe That Conquered an Empire

A small Spanish band led by the captain Pizarro approached the Inca empire with horses, swords, and firearms, but their real weapon turned out to be invisible. Smallpox had already arrived in South America via the trading routes from Panama, and the pathogen had decimated the Inca population, including the reigning emperor and his immediate successor, before any Spaniard had even reached the capital. The empire was therefore convulsed by a succession crisis and a depopulated military when Pizarro presented himself. Pizarro captured the new emperor Atahualpa and demanded a ransom of one entire room filled to the ceiling with gold. The Incas delivered the gold. Pizarro accepted the ransom and then executed Atahualpa anyway. The conquest of the largest empire in the western hemisphere had been completed by a contagion riding ahead of the men who eventually accepted credit for the victory.

**Q1:** Which Spanish captain led the small band that captured the Inca emperor, demanded a roomful of gold as ransom, and executed him after collecting it?
**A1:** Pizarro

**Q2:** Which viral contagion, arriving via Panama trade routes ahead of any Spaniard, decimated the Inca population and convulsed the empire before the conquest itself began?
**A2:** smallpox

**Q3:** Which captured Inca emperor was executed by Pizarro despite having delivered a room filled to the ceiling with gold as the demanded ransom?
**A3:** Atahualpa

## 7.10  The Silver Mountain at the Sky

In the high thin air of the Bolivian altiplano stands a mountain called Potosí, beneath which lay one of the richest concentrations of silver ore the planet has ever yielded. Spanish authorities forced indigenous Quechua and African slave labourers into the oxygen-thin tunnels to extract the metal, and uncounted thousands perished underground from the combination of altitude, mercury exposure, and exhaustion. The silver was loaded onto enormous Spanish galleons and shipped first to Spain and then onward to Manila in the Philippines, where it was exchanged for Chinese tea, porcelain, and silk. The Potosí trade therefore constituted the first genuinely global commercial network in human history, the lethal underground at one end of the chain financing the porcelain teacup that arrived eventually at the other.

**Q1:** Which Bolivian mountain, in the thin air of the altiplano, yielded one of the richest concentrations of silver ore the planet has ever produced?
**A1:** Potosí

**Q2:** Which precious metal, extracted by Quechua and African slave labour from the Potosí tunnels, was shipped on Spanish galleons across the Pacific to Manila?
**A2:** silver

**Q3:** Which Philippine port served as the Asian terminus of the Potosí silver route, where Spanish galleons exchanged metal for Chinese tea, porcelain, and silk?
**A3:** Manila

## 7.11  The Tubers and the Cocoa Bean

The Andes had cultivated the potato for thousands of years before the Spanish encountered it, and the Spanish hauled the tubers home as a curiosity that gradually transformed into the staple food of every European peasant who could not afford bread. The potato proved so productive that the population of northern Europe expanded rapidly until a single fungal blight crossed the Atlantic and infected the Irish crop, decimating the population through starvation and forcing two million survivors to migrate westward across the same ocean to the Americas. The cocoa bean had been ceremonial currency among the Aztec elite, valuable enough that a sack of beans could purchase a slave. The Spanish added sugar to the bitter Aztec drink, sweetened the resulting beverage, exported it to Europe, and the cocoa bean was transformed into the chocolate bar that no civilisation now imagines doing without.

**Q1:** Which Andean tuber, originally cultivated for thousands of years before the Spanish arrived, transformed into the staple of every European peasant who could not afford bread?
**A1:** potato

**Q2:** Which fungal disease decimated the Irish potato crop, killed many through starvation, and forced two million survivors to migrate westward across the Atlantic?
**A2:** potato blight

**Q3:** Which Aztec ceremonial currency, sweetened by the Spanish, was eventually transformed into the chocolate bar that no civilisation now imagines doing without?
**A3:** cocoa bean

## 7.12  The Modern Reckoning

The Amazon rainforest, often called the lungs of the planet, is being cleared rapidly at the present moment to make room for cattle ranches and soybean fields exporting to the global meat trade. Colombian cocaine, refined from the coca leaf that Andean peoples have chewed ceremonially for thousands of years, generated drug lords wealthier than the governments that attempted to prosecute them. Football has become the third major South American export to the world after the potato and the chocolate bar: Brazilians treat the sport as a national religion, the Argentines paint Maradona's notorious Hand of God goal onto murals in every city, and the continent that gave the world the potato may yet give it the only universal language nobody resists speaking.

**Q1:** Which immense South American rainforest, the lungs of the planet, is being cleared rapidly for cattle ranches and soybean fields exporting to the global meat trade?
**A1:** Amazon

**Q2:** Which Andean plant, ceremonially chewed by indigenous peoples for thousands of years, is refined in Colombia into cocaine that has generated drug lords wealthier than the governments?
**A2:** coca leaf

**Q3:** Which Argentine football player is famous for the notorious Hand of God goal, painted onto murals across his home country?
**A3:** Maradona
